In this post I'm going to go about some things that have happened in the first 40 days of my biggest project ever, Polaris.

So first of, one thing I've been working on is that this project actually receives some identity beyond "self improvement". That's vague and it doesn't really tell people what it is that I do, because I do a lot. I did this by putting more effort into an unconventional project, at least by my means. Atlas.

Atlas is a project about creating and completing tasks, trips, and missions known as "Quests". Quests can range from going somewhere, meeting someone, doing something bold or fun, or engaging in an activity with a clear goal in mind. Every Quest needs to be one or more of the following: Memorable, important for my growth, fun, or difficult. After completing a quest, the Quester feels fulfilled, proud of what they've done, or happy that they could experience a little more of life. Moreover, Quests can also be life goals that one works towards. There are also Odysseys, which are collections of multiple Quests usually done in quick succession, as well as Journeys, which are collections of Odysseys. Every completed Quest, Odyssey, and Journey yield a reward in cosmic needles. These can range from a tiny quest for no needles at all, to the average short quest of one needle, to even huge quests worth tens of cosmic needles. All in all, Atlas compels the Quester to gather new experiences, learn new things, and to take pride in their self-competitive identity.

Soon I will try to put a bigger emphasis on STARR (as it was originally designed to be the mascot project, a.k.a the one you think of when you think about Polaris). After that, it might be good to do Polaris itself justice, as its shtick isn't just being the parent of the aforementioned.

In these fourty days, I've had lots of other successes too. I've earned my spark for doing what I do back successfully, which has a lot to do with Atlas having more identity and being much more fun than Project Star's stoic and disciplinary nature for example, but also with the changes and additions that this new era has brought me. Ever since I started Polaris I've been using Obsidian more and ever since I've been using Obsidian more I've been more organised. More efficient and much more oriented towards what I should do, want to do, and need to do.

I don't forget what I have to do anymore, and coming up with ideas is much easier once you get in a creative headspace too. Not to mention that being pushed to do so many new things has also opened me up to more abstract ideas. Before the first cycle's 27% phase, about 100 days in, I was never a creative person. I was quite boring in fact. Then I tried writing, and poetry, and music, and lots of other things and I found out that I have been creative all this time, I just didn't use my potential. Using that same principle I've forced myself to do things outside of my comfort zone, but not in completely unknown territory.

I picked up the guitar as an instrument. Since I was a child I wasn't known for having the best motoric skills and I regularly avoided drawing, DIY-ing and stuff like that because my hands weren't cut out for it. (Off topic, but I have been called "motorically retarded" by someone as a joke in school a couple months back because I can't even throw a ball to save my life). This new instrument on the other hand is very motorically demanding and requires me to reopen my mind to the way I think about musical theory as someone who also plays the piano and produces music (which is piano-based).

In the same way, I've also been practicing crocheting a little on the side, though I haven't had much time. Time will tell how quickly I progress with that, especially because I have tried multiple times before. Multiple people who do it very well have also tried to explain it to me, but I simply couldn't get into it, partly maybe because of my loser-mindset when it comes to these things. It just seems out of reach that this thing which is all about my biggest weakness would be something I can learn, but alas, I'm gonna have to eventually.

Thirdly, and this one is a bit different from the other two, I dyed and cut my hair differently than I usually do. I've had two different haircuts in my life, one of which I wasn't too fond of and one which I outright disliked. The last time I've had short hair was I believe in fifth or sixth grade, and the last time I didn't have cherry red hair was a year or two ago, so it's a big change. Done in the matter of one day completely spontaneously too, for better or for worse. Anyone who knows me and what I worry about even half well knows that I'm very specific about my hair, and it used to be even worse. I used to avoid school because my hair looked stupid. I used to stay up for hours staring at the mirror wondering why I look like a homeless ungroomed dog. Last week I cut my hair myself making it much shorter than I was comfortable with because I needed a drastic change. Yesterday, I doubled down on that same concept. In a way those two actions were some of the best things I've done these last few years concerning my self-image. I don't really care about my hair anymore, in fact I'm happy. I'm happy that it's different, that I'm a new person now. Just have to get used to it.
