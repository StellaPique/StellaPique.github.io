# What's Up?

I graduated. I celebrated. And while I celebrated, some mildly interesting things happened that I can't share here. Quite frankly there isn't much I can really say here about yesterday. But I can say, my attitude towards certain things changed in some aspects, and was amplified in others.

I have so, so much love to give. For good people, for bad, even horrible people. For people I like, and for people I don't like either. There's such a weird charm about being forced to endure years with people you know you will never really talk to, people you will never like, people you will never not find annoying, as long as there are people you can lean back on. The beauty of school has always been quite apparent to me. It's the lack of freedom that is so interesting in a way. You are put into your class without choice. You can meet new people, you can meet new teachers, you might even re-meet people you haven't seen since kindergarten, since pre-school, since whatever.

My position and reputation within my class has always been kind of interesting, though definitely heavily disadvantaged. I've been in five different schools in the last ten years, and subsequently at least just as many classes, though I did have to stay behind one time, leaving me where I was until yesterday.

With this in mind, I was also an obvious outsider for more than 75% of the time there, and less obviously one for the remainder of the time. I'm extremely sociable. I'll talk with anyone, from any age group, with any kind of mindset, though it wasn't always this way. In fact, it hasn't even been a year. Swift, or what I call my former self, used to go months and months without talking to anyone at all. Maybe the short "good morning" to family members, or maybe answering a question when someone asked me something in school. It's kind of fascinating if I'm allowed to say so myself, how quickly I changed from one polar extreme to the other.

Though this didn't really give me much time to form friendships, not just in this last sociable phase, but in every class. That's being in a class for 2 years on average as a person with bad social skills, and people around me who don't make it easy to get close to them. The thing is, I'm not on the same path as most people and I've never been. Most people, at least those which I meet have established friends. They've had them. For a year, or two, or four, or eight, and leaning towards the latter.

With all this in mind, I can safely say I didn't experience a normal childhood, and I can say with certainty that I am, most of all and everything, an outsider. In great aspects, and in bad ones. I have a friend I really do trust who knows me pretty pretty well, but that's about it. Other people I only just "know" or am friendly towards. Don't get me wrong, it's lovely being greeted wherever I go (in my school's places), and it does really help keep me afloat, to not feel so lonely, but it's not all I need.

It was nice to know so many people though, even if just on a surface level. There were so, so many people I admired, and still admire. I was also able to forgive Pinkie. I know she's not evil, she's just young and too stupid and scummy not to do bad things. I forgive her best friend too for how she treated me, and I forgive the 206th for how he did. I've left the class behind with pretty much no conflicts.

All I feel towards everyone and anyone at all can only be described with one word. Love.
