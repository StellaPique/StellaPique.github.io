This is a short research article about Avatar: The Last Airbender's four elements and how it can help you learn more about yourself and the world.

## Introduction

After watching Avatar: The Last Airbender and seeing the show's carefully thought out philosophical systems and worldbuilding, I have realized that a lot of the relevant concepts apply not just to the kids' show, but also to us. The four elements are an amazing representation of four different types of personalities that occur both through genetics and nurturing. As a person's personality develops, they usually fall into one of the four categories. As they inherit the strengths, the corresponding natural weaknesses follow. A kind person is prone to be used, a confident person is prone to be egoistic, and so on.

Just as Fire & Water or Air & Earth sit as opposites within the show's elemental system, the traits they represent — passion and adaptability, rigidity and freedom — sit as opposites within us. Recognizing this pattern showed me the bad in my good, and the good in my bad.

Most personality frameworks, like Myers-Briggs, sort people into types without explaining why each type struggles where it does. The Avatar elemental system offers something more useful: it pairs each strength with its corresponding weakness, so understanding your type also means understanding exactly where it will trip you up.

There are four major elements / bending styles in the Avatar universe. These four elements are *Fire*, *Air*, *Water*, and *Earth* in that exact order. With each element also come personality traits, both positive and negative. Every single one of them teaches the user a unique lesson that shapes their character, their habits, and their strengths and weaknesses. It is the combination of all four elements in one person that makes the avatar so powerful in the show universe. Just the same, it can make anyone else, even you, more well-rounded too, as the elements are not only bending, but also the overlooked philosophies behind them.

## Jing

Before I begin explaining each element, it is important to know about *Jing*. Jing is a term that describes the myriad of options one has during battle on how to direct their energy, both internally and externally. Positive Jing is doing, negative Jing is avoiding, neutral Jing is waiting and listening. It is important to understand one's natural tendencies and why you have them if you want to grow.

Firebenders mostly employ positive Jing, as it is the element of activity.
Airbenders mostly employ negative Jing, as it is the element of avoidance.
Waterbenders mostly employ positive & negative Jing, as it is the element of change.
Earthbenders mostly employ neutral Jing, as it is the element of patience and perseverance.

## The Four Elements

### Fire

Fire is the first of the four elements in multiple ways, and as we go from element to element you will understand why the order exists and matters. The in-universe reason is that the first Avatar, Avatar Wan was given and learned Firebending first. It acted as a huge stepping stone for humanity, just like the discovery of fire in the real world. Therefore, Fire is the element of action, passion, confidence, and the act of moving forward. Users draw their power from their breath, a metaphor for the fact that Firebending is the only one with an internal source. Users are prone to losing control of the power of the flame. Passion can be twisted into hatred and aggression, and confidence into pride. Fire is the natural opposite of Water.

When something is not going according to plan, a Firebender will push through, applying more force, more will, more direct action until the obstacle gives way. This works when the obstacle can be overpowered. It fails when it can't, and pushing harder only makes things worse, such as through overexertion.

### Air

Air is the second element in the cycle. It is the element of freedom, creativity, and avoidance. This is clearly seen in the Airbenders' culture in-universe. As pacifists, they avoid fighting if they can, and try to live without worldly attachments. Things such as greed, close-mindedness and rigidity are things users generally do not struggle with. Users are prone to avoiding their problems by trying to find a way around when courage or persistence is what is actually needed. Air is the natural opposite of Earth.

Air is learned after Fire to pair its passion with humility and direction. Passion without a compass causes harm even when it means well.

When something is not going according to plan, an Airbender will step back, disengage, or find another route entirely rather than confront it directly.

### Water

Water is the element of change, direction, flow, and adaptability. Doing the most of what you have instead of building a solution from the ground up. Where Fire creates its own momentum, Water moves with what's already there, redirecting rather than generating force. This is what makes them opposites: not strength against weakness, but two different relationships to power itself. Users are prone to struggling when there is nothing already in motion to redirect. Without existing external momentum, direction, or a problem already in progress, adaptability has nothing to work with.

Water is learned after Air to ground its freedom in responsiveness. Flexibility without direction can drift aimlessly. For example, someone might stretch themselves thin doing a lot of different things at once. Water provides enough direction to cut what is unneeded.

When something is not going according to plan, a Waterbender would consider what is working and what is not, and adapt accordingly.

### Earth

Earth is the element of substance, strength and patience. Where Air would try to find a creative way to go around, Earth faces problems head on. This is clearly seen in Earthbenders' culture in-universe. Earthbenders, hardened by war and survival, don't act until they see an opportunity. Earth is the element of endurance: not the fastest solution, but the one that holds. Users are prone to being stubborn and rigid, mistaking close-mindedness for strength even when the situation has already changed around them. Persistence can be twisted into refusal, and patience into inaction. Earth is the natural opposite of Air.

Earth is learned after Water to ground its adaptability in something stable. Constant redirection without a fixed foundation can leave a person without any position of their own, and Earth gives that flexibility something solid to stand on.

When something is not going according to plan, an Earthbender will hold their ground, wait, and outlast the problem rather than force or dodge it. This works when the situation rewards patience. It fails when the situation has already moved on and holding still just means getting left behind.

## Similarities & Parallels

Each of the four elements draws clear references to the others. Water is external adaptation where Fire is internal strength, and Air looks for a way around where Earth faces problems directly. These relations however do not stop at natural opposites. Earth and Fire are both strong-willed in their own ways, whether persistent or passionate. Air and Water are both open to new ideas, whether creative or adaptable. A link exists between every single combination of two elements whether it is an opposite or similar trait that connects them.

It is good to learn the elements in the correct order especially because of the transition from your native element to your natural opposite. The element after your native in the natural order of the four elements (Fire, Air, Water, Earth) shares traits with your opposite to ease you in.

## How You Can Learn From The Others

As stated, you don't have to be the literal bender of all four elements to learn the philosophies behind them. Knowing where you stand can help you understand yourself, and be more proud of the things that make you, you, instead of resenting them. Every person has their own way of solving problems. Understanding this will help you see things from a new perspective, and help you grow as a person. A person with teachings of only one element, whether it be Fire, Air, Water or Earth tends to see things in a narrower point of view than someone drawing on two or more.
